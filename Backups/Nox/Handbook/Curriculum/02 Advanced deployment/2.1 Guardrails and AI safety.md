# Guardrails & AI safety

## Purpose
Map practical controls to risks before, during, and after an agent's task.

## Learning objectives
- Identify boundaries for data, tools, and actions.
- Place preventive, in-process, and output checks in a workflow.
- Define stop conditions and an escalation path.

## Lesson
Guardrails limit what an agent can access, decide, or do. Start by describing the task and its risks: what information is sensitive, which actions have external effects, and what a harmful or incorrect outcome would look like.

Controls can operate at several points. **Before** work, constrain task, tools, and data. **During** execution, validate inputs, restrict action scope, and stop on unexpected states. **After** execution, review outputs and record appropriate actions. Prompt instructions alone do not replace technical permissions or human review where those are needed.

Make controls specific and testable. “Be careful” is hard to enforce; “do not send a message without a named reviewer's approval” is observable. Define what happens when evidence is missing, prohibited data appears, or the agent reaches an action outside its authority. Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive information in logs.

## Key takeaways
- Limit tools to what the task needs.
- Check outputs before they reach a customer.
- Keep an appropriate record so mistakes can be traced.

## Try it
Write three actions your agent must not take without approval. For each, state a preventive control, the approval signal, and what it should do if approval is unavailable.

## Check your understanding
1. Give one example of a preventive guardrail and one output check.
2. What should an agent do when it reaches an action outside its authority?
