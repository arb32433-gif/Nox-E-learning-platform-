# Human-in-the-loop

## Purpose
Place human review at decisions where judgment, authority, or impact warrants it.

## Learning objectives
- Distinguish automatic actions from those needing approval.
- Design a concise, informed approval request.
- Use review outcomes to improve the workflow.

## Lesson
Human-in-the-loop means a person participates at a meaningful decision point. Review is especially useful before actions that are hard to reverse, affect another person, spend money, publish information, or rely on ambiguous evidence.

An approval request should show the proposed action, relevant context, likely impact, and options to approve, edit, or stop. Give the reviewer enough evidence to decide without reconstructing the entire run. If information is missing, pause and ask; do not treat silence as consent.

Not every step needs a person. Automate low-risk, reversible work when boundaries are clear and the workflow has been tested. Sample automated outcomes and examine where people edit or reject proposals; repeated corrections may reveal unclear instructions or weak evidence.

## Key takeaways
- Approve high-impact actions; automate low-risk ones with clear boundaries.
- Make each proposal quick to understand and act on.
- Track review outcomes to improve the workflow.

## Try it
Choose one action that should always require approval and one that could run automatically. Explain impact, reversibility, and what information a reviewer or monitor needs.

## Check your understanding
1. What information should an approval request contain?
2. Why should silence not count as approval?
