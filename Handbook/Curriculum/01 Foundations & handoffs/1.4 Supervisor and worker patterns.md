# Supervisor & worker patterns

## Purpose
Choose and scope delegated subtasks in a supervised workflow.

## Learning objectives
- Describe supervisor and worker responsibilities.
- Split a project into bounded, reviewable subtasks.
- Plan how results return for validation.

## Lesson
In this pattern, a supervisor interprets the objective, creates a plan, assigns bounded subtasks, and integrates results. Worker agents carry out assigned pieces and return outputs with evidence and limitations. The supervisor remains responsible for coherence and the final response.

A good subtask has a clear input, specific output, and checkable boundary. For example, a worker can extract dates from supplied documents and cite source passages; the supervisor can compare findings and draft a summary. Avoid vague or overlapping assignments that rely on hidden context.

Delegation adds coordination costs. Use it when work can be separated or checked independently. The supervisor detects missing pieces, resolves conflicts, and asks for human input when uncertainty or impact exceeds the workflow's limits.

## Key takeaways
- Subtasks should be small enough to check.
- The supervisor owns the final answer.
- Workers carry out tasks; they do not set the overall plan.

## Try it
Break a project into five subtasks. For each, write the worker's input, expected output, and review check. Choose one low-risk subtask to delegate first.

## Check your understanding
1. What makes a worker subtask easy to review?
2. Who should reconcile conflicting worker results?
